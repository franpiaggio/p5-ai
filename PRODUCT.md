# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Creative coders and developers curious about AI-assisted editing. Two contexts:

- **App users**: people sketching in p5.js who want an assistant that edits
  their running sketch. They range from hobbyists trying a demo to developers
  bringing their own API keys. They judge results visually and in seconds.
- **Blog readers**: engineers arriving from LinkedIn/X posts, reading
  long-form writeups about how the editor works (edit formats, matching
  cascades, token economics). They read on laptops and phones, usually once,
  and decide quickly whether the author knows what they are talking about.

## Product Purpose

An AI-powered p5.js editor: describe a change in chat and it arrives as a
reviewable diff over the live sketch. The project doubles as a public
experiment. The blog documents the techniques (search/replace formats, fuzzy
matching, fragment guards, caching) so the writing itself demonstrates the
engineering quality of the tool. Success: the demo feels reliable, and the
writeups earn trust from technical readers.

## Brand Personality

Ingenieril, sobrio, confiable. Engineering-first voice in the style of a
serious engineering blog: measured claims backed by measurements, real
screenshots over staged mockups, honesty about limitations and trade-offs.
The creative-coding subject matter provides the color; the presentation stays
calm and does not perform.

## Anti-references

- AI-generated landing-page slop: gradient text, hero metrics, icon card
  grids, hype adjectives, em-dash-riddled prose.
- SaaS template aesthetics (centered stack hero, purple gradients).
- Costume "hacker" styling: monospace everywhere, neon-on-black terminal
  cosplay.
- Overwrought editorial-magazine affectation (italic display serifs, drop
  caps) on what is fundamentally an engineering essay.

## Design Principles

1. **The technique is the story.** Every visual choice serves comprehension:
   diagrams, code, and screenshots are first-class content, never decoration.
2. **Show the real thing.** Screenshots come from the running app; examples
   come from real incidents. Nothing staged, nothing invented.
3. **Legibility over ornament.** Long-form surfaces optimize for sustained
   reading: measured line lengths, quiet chrome, clear hierarchy.
4. **Honest about limits.** Caveats and failure modes are stated plainly;
   refusing to guess is presented as a feature, in the UI and in the prose.
5. **Calm confidence.** No exclamation marks, no urgency, no persuasion
   theater. The work speaks in a normal tone of voice.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as the baseline: contrast ratios, keyboard reachability, alt
text on all informative images. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Body text
never below 16px on any surface.
